# Voice and content

Miltinson copy sounds like one person who builds things and tells you the truth about them. Every
string in a product — button labels, empty states, error messages — is brand surface.

## The voice

- **I speak as myself.** First person singular — "I build software, teach AI, and
  create resources for coaches." Never the royal "we"; this isn't a company of many.
- **The reader is "you."** Direct address, no buffer.
- **Confident but unguarded.** The field is full of nonsense and the brand explicitly
  opts out: "No jargon, no judgment — just practical solutions."

## Tone, in order of weight

1. **Practical** — what's actually useful, what saves real time.
2. **Honest** — call out hype, show real prices, admit limitations.
3. **Warm** — patient with non-technical readers; a trusted hand, not a gatekeeper.
4. **Quietly confident** — never flex credentials; let the work speak.

## Mechanics

- **Sentence case** for headings — "Need help with AI or tech?", never ALL CAPS body
  content. Title Case only for product names and the tagline.
- **Em-dashes** are the favored break — like this one.
- **Oxford commas**, yes.
- **Pricing prefixed with "From"** — "From $150/hr", never a single firm number.
- **The wordmark takes its period.** "Miltinson." — the dot is part of the mark, in
  amber.

## Words

Use: build, ship, practical, honest, no-fluff, hands-on, real, useful, clear, simple.

Avoid: synergy, leverage, unlock, empower, robust, cutting-edge, revolutionary,
world-class, seamless, frictionless, reimagine, 10x, ninja, rockstar — and "we" when I
mean "I".

## Emoji and icons

No decorative emoji in headings, body copy, or system UI. A checkmark (✓) is fine in
plain-text checklists. When UI needs a small mark, use a Lucide icon at 1.5px stroke,
inheriting `currentColor`, paired with a text label in navigation and primary actions.

## In components

- Button labels are verbs: "Save changes", "Send message".
- Empty states say what goes here and how to add it — see
  [EmptyState](/components/empty-state).
- Error messages name the problem and the fix, in that order, without blame.
- Alerts and toasts get one sentence; if it needs two, it needs a page.
